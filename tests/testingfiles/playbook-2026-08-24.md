Market Expectations for Mid-August: Every time we get a little bit of positive news, there’s something (or someone) that offsets it. Unfortunately, we don’t see that changing until one of two things happens, and neither are likely in the short term. Either there is regime change in Iran or Trump just absolutely folds. Trump folding is the more likely of the two, but if he makes it through mid-terms maintaining control of Congress then all bets are off on that. This will bleed into 2028 if that’s the case. But at that point, when it comes to the market, will it really even matter, or will it be just something in the background that keeps oil prices elevated but global economies have adapted to? We think that will probably be the case. Each kinetic exchange, each vitriolic statement having less of an impact on markets. Believing that, we continue to press forward focusing on the current trends, both intermediate and macro, and they are still up. Bond yields are at 20 yr highs, fuel prices are what they are, inflation is what it is… some of the AI sector names had close to a 50% retracements, and the equity markets are firmly in an uptrend. You can’t fight that; you can’t position yourself for the “next shoe to drop” (you can but we don’t see that making you any more money than just going to a casino). So we press forward with the majority of our sector outlooks as Bullish. Circling back to bonds, we love bonds at these levels and if you have been following the Investor Trend Suggestions you have been a happy camper. We don’t see that changing, in fact we would suggest rebalancing portfolios to place a higher weight on Treasuries at these levels (Bessent’s Treasury Dept isn’t going to let yield continue to rise unchecked). So, where does that leave us? Equity markets going higher, bond prices stabilizing. The question now is leadership. What sector will perform the least amount of stress on the holder? We still believe its midcap stocks. We think tech/AI is played out. We see a rotation out of tech and into small to midcap stocks (IWM), especially if rates are going to hold these levels or drop. That said, IWM doesn’t have the “explosiveness” of tech. An IWM lead market would continue to trend higher on a macro basis but without the double-digit gains. Avoiding the conditional Bull Call spread executions may still be the right move for now, but selling OTM puts seems as solid as ever. As for Commodities: Keep accumulating TLT via selling quarterly puts. Precious metals: the neutral bullish stance remains, expect large swings to continue, go wide and hide. Crude oil: The highs are in. Swings will continue based on last headline, but the “shock” price when this first began is unlikely to be retested. In summary, expect little to occur the rest of the summer, Q3 might bring us some action… and we’ll deal with it then.
